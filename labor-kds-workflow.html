Labor Productivity
& KDS Workflow

Use this to separate a true labor problem from a workflow problem. More people do not automatically create more throughput, and faster tickets do not automatically mean the right work is being prioritized.

1. Demand & Deployment

Staffing is tied to demand by daypart.Schedules reflect expected transactions, sales, production load, and channel mix—not just historical habit.
Peak deployment positions are defined before the rush.Leaders are not deciding station ownership after the queue has already formed.
Leaders know which position is the constraint.The team can identify the bottleneck station instead of adding labor everywhere.

2. Prep Readiness

Prep levels are connected to expected demand.Critical ingredients, dough, sauces, toppings, packaging, and smallwares are available without excessive overproduction.
Pre-rush readiness is verified.A manager confirms critical station setup rather than assuming the checklist was completed.
Stockouts and emergency prep are recorded.Recurring interruptions become forecast/training issues to solve, not normal rush behavior.

3. KDS & Ticket Sequencing

Every station sees the information it needs.Items, modifiers, priority, order channel, and timing cues are visible without repeated verbal translation.
Bump behavior matches physical completion.Tickets are not cleared early just to improve displayed speed.
Rush prioritization rules are explicit.Large orders, delivery, slices, dine-in, and pickup do not compete through improvised ticket jumping.

4. Bottleneck Response

The manager can see queue buildup early.Trigger points exist for redeployment before wait time becomes guest recovery.
Cross-trained support has a defined destination.When help is added, the person relieves the constraint rather than adding congestion.
Root cause is separated from symptom.Equipment, prep, skill, sequencing, order mix, staffing, and handoff delays are categorized differently.

5. Handoff & Productivity Review

Finished product does not wait unnecessarily for handoff.Production completion and pickup release are treated as separate control points.
Labor review includes throughput, not only labor percentage.Leaders look at what the hours produced—not just whether the percentage hit target.
Peak misses create a documented next action.The next shift starts with a changed control, not the same explanation.
Stabilis rule: Labor productivity is a system outcome. Before cutting hours or adding people, identify whether the constraint is demand planning, prep, station design, skill, KDS sequencing, or handoff.

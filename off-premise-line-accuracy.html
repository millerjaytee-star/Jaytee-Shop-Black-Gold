Off-Premise &
Line Accuracy

Use this to pressure-test the flow from order entry to handoff. Public complaints can point to a symptom, but only internal observation and operating data can confirm the root cause.

1. Order Intake & Routing

Every order source enters a defined production flow.POS, app, kiosk, delivery aggregator, catering, and phone orders do not create competing unofficial queues.
Modifiers are visible at the station that must execute them.No dependence on memory or verbal relay for allergies, removals, sides, sauces, or substitutions.
Priority rules are clear during peak.Leaders know how to manage dine-in, pickup, delivery, and large-order timing without random ticket jumping.

2. Production & Expo Ownership

One person owns final order verification during peak.Assembly ownership is not diffused across whoever happens to touch the bag last.
Completed items are staged by order, not by convenience.Drinks, sides, condiments, desserts, and modifiers stay connected to the correct ticket.
Missing-item exceptions are resolved before handoff.The expo owner has authority and a clear escalation path instead of sending an incomplete order.

3. Bag Verification

Bag count is reconciled to the order.Multi-bag orders use a visible count or label convention.
High-risk items receive a defined verification step.Beverages, sauces, sides, modifiers, utensils, and special instructions are not left to chance.
Sealing happens after verification.The process does not make correction harder by sealing too early.

4. Pickup & Driver Handoff

Pickup location and ownership are obvious.Drivers and guests know where to go without interrupting production.
Order identity is confirmed at handoff.Name/order number and bag count are matched before release.
Driver wait is separated from kitchen delay.Leaders can distinguish early drivers, dispatch mismatch, production delay, and staging delay.

5. Error Learning Loop

Remakes/refunds are categorized by reason.Wrong item, missing item, modifier, quality, temperature, delay, packaging, and delivery issues are distinguishable.
Recurring patterns reach the manager review.Problems do not disappear into guest recovery without an operating response.
Corrective actions are verified.A new checklist or coaching conversation counts only when the behavior is later observed working.
Stabilis rule: Order accuracy is not one employee's memory problem. It is a chain of control points. Diagnose which control failed before adding more labor or another checklist.

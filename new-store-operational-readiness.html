New Store
Operational Readiness

Use this before opening a new location—or when a recent opening still feels dependent on heroic effort. The goal is not perfection. The goal is to make the controls visible enough that leadership knows what is ready, what is fragile, and who owns the gap.

1. Leadership & Ownership

GM ownership is explicit.The location leader knows the KPIs, decisions, escalation thresholds, and recurring reviews they own.
Every critical opening task has an owner and due date.No shared-accountability language such as “the team will handle it.”
Manager coverage is mapped to actual demand.Opening week does not rely on permanent overstaffing by senior leaders.

2. Labor & Training

Labor plan is built from expected demand, not copied from another store.Opening assumptions are documented so they can be adjusted quickly.
Role certification is complete for critical stations.Training completion is verified by demonstrated performance, not attendance alone.
Peak deployment has a written plan.Leader, expo, production, register, pickup, and recovery ownership are clear.

3. Inventory, Waste & Purchasing

Opening pars have an owner and review date.Pars are treated as starting hypotheses and adjusted against real usage.
Receiving and transfer controls are live.Quantity, quality, invoice, transfer, and discrepancy responsibility is defined.
Waste/remake reasons are categorized.Leaders can distinguish training, prep, forecasting, quality, and order-accuracy issues.

4. Shift Execution

Open, handoff, peak, and close routines are standardized.Managers do not invent a different operating rhythm each shift.
Service exceptions have an escalation path.The team knows when to recover, when to involve leadership, and how to record the issue.
Off-premise handoff is owned.Pickup shelves, drivers, modifiers, beverages, sides, and bag verification have clear control points.

5. First-30-Day Control

Daily opening-week scorecard exists.Track only the few signals leadership must see every day.
Exceptions turn into actions.Every material miss gets an owner, due date, correction, and verification step.
A 7/14/30-day review is scheduled before opening.The operating model gets recalibrated using actual performance instead of memory.
Stabilis rule: A new store is not operationally ready because the doors can open. It is ready when ordinary leaders can run the standards, see exceptions, and close the loop without constant founder intervention.
